Literature review

Knobloch Syndrome and Amniotic Membrane Grafting

Evidence underpinning a case of bilateral macular hole–related retinal detachment managed with dual-site amniotic membrane grafting.

Overview

Knobloch syndrome is a rare autosomal recessive vitreoretinopathy caused by biallelic COL18A1 variants. Its ocular burden is dominated by early, frequently bilateral retinal detachment, a third to more than half of it macular hole–related. Surgical outcomes are poor, and the field has moved quickly toward biological grafts as a rescue strategy.

Three points the literature establishes

Grafting has become routine within two years

Before 2024 there was no cohort-level evidence. Özdek's 34-patient series changed that: sealing grafts in 11 of 16 macular hole detachments, with reattachment of 82% against 40% 11. Two individual case reports preceded it 1415, both unilateral and both at the macula.

The internal limiting membrane usually cannot be peeled

Peeling was feasible in only 2 of 16 macular hole eyes, both in older children aged 4 and 10 11. By contrast, extended peeling in general paediatric detachment is reported as no harder than in adults 42 — so unpeelability here is disorder-specific, not an age effect.

Anatomical success does not deliver vision

Mean acuity was 1.2 logMAR before and 1.3 after successful repair 10. Visual potential is typically 20/200 to 20/400 even in eyes that never detach, because of macular atrophy 11. Grafting for complex detachment produced no significant acuity gain 29.

Contraction is the failure mode that matters

Dislocation or contracture affects 3.6% of grafts in pooled series 20, rising to 25% in complex paediatric detachment 29. It caused outright failure of a single-piece graft at two weeks in the only directly comparable Knobloch case 15. The cryopreserved subgroup of that meta-analysis had a lower rate, 3% (95% CI 0–10) against 6% (0–15) pooled, though the intervals overlap and the authors put it no more strongly than cryopreserved grafts “might have better outcomes” 22.

Scope

PubMed and MEDLINE searches returned 106 Knobloch records, 63 COL18A1 records with retinal terms, and 108 amniotic membrane records with retinal terms in the title. These were supplemented by Consensus searches across Semantic Scholar, Scopus, PubMed and arXiv, and by hand-searching the reference lists of the three principal cohorts 91011. Forty-six references are retained; nine were read in full text. Literature current to August 2026.

Two cautions before quoting figures. Refractive and axial-length values from Alzaben 10 were extracted from a PDF text layer in which minus signs are lost — the myopic direction is unambiguous from context, but each figure should be checked against the typeset article. And the 82% versus 40% graft advantage 11 carries P = 0.09 across 16 eyes; it is a near-doubling that is underpowered, not an established effect.

Three patterns run through the register. Grafts at the macula seal reliably but rarely improve acuity — Almasri's unchanged 20/2000 14 is the clearest illustration, Ownagh's 20/70 39 the clearest exception. Single-piece subretinal grafts failed by contracture in the one directly comparable Knobloch case, and multilayer or sandwich constructions were the salvage 15. And extramacular use, while established in pathologic myopia and PVR 282930, has not previously been reported in Knobloch syndrome.

The Disorder

Knobloch syndrome (KNO1, OMIM 267750) was described in 1971 as high myopia, vitreoretinal degeneration with retinal detachment, and occipital skull defects 1, and attributed to COL18A1 three decades later 2. Half a century on, each element of that triad is less reliable than the definition implies.

Occipital defects are neither necessary nor common enough to rely on

Documented in 44% of Alzaben's 50 patients 10 and absent in two-thirds of Özdek's cohort 11. Half of Khan's genetically confirmed children had no clinically discernible defect 3.

The ocular phenotype alone is diagnostic

Khan identified as pathognomonic the combination of smooth cryptless irides, ectopia lentis, and a distinctive vitreoretinal degeneration — diffuse severe RPE atrophy with prominent choroidal vessel show, macular atrophic lesions often punched-out, and white fibrillar vitreous condensations 3.

The mechanism is basement-membrane failure, not stretching

Collagen XVIII anchors vitreous collagen fibrils to the internal limiting membrane 78. Its loss produces an abnormally adherent vitreoretinal interface and predisposes to detachment independent of the degree of myopia 10 — which is why the macular hole phenotype appears in eyes that are not axially elongated.

Misdiagnosis is common

Reported as familial exudative vitreoretinopathy 16, as albinism 44, and as uveitis with exudative detachment 11. Esotropia and nystagmus are a recognised presenting picture 11.

Is White 2017 relevant to the present case?

Yes, but narrowly — as a supporting citation, not a comparator. White and colleagues describe two siblings with COL18A1 variants, polymicrogyria on MRI and no occipital defects; the 7-month-old had a serous detachment, not a rhegmatogenous macular hole detachment, and received no graft 17. It does not belong in the case-comparison tables.

It earns its place as one of the strongest citations for the proposition that occipital defects may be absent while central nervous system involvement is present. Together with Caglayan 19 it is the basis for recommending brain imaging in all COL18A1 patients. It should not be used to support any claim about grafting, macular holes or surgical outcome.

The prophylaxis question

Alzaben records that none of the 50 patients underwent prophylactic intervention, and notes that prophylaxis “obviously would not be feasible” for the third of detachments caused by macular holes 10. Özdek does not advocate routine peripheral laser in the absence of visible lattice, holes or breaks, on the grounds that laser-induced contraction of an already pathological hyaloid may itself trigger hole formation 11. Moysidis has argued for prophylactic buckling 12. Any reviewer will raise this; in the present case both eyes were already detached at presentation.

Reading the present case against this table

Hyperopia in Knobloch syndrome is reported, but only unilaterally, in anisometropic patients, and with an identified mechanism 4. Bilateral symmetrical hyperopia present before detachment was recognised, and sufficient to produce fully accommodative esotropia, is not described.

Biometry identifies the mechanism, and it is not a short eye. Axial length was unremarkable and, if anything, overestimated by measurement through silicone oil — corroborated independently by the intraoperative lens recalculation after oil removal, which required a lens 4 D stronger than predicted.

Both corneas were flat, and the two eyes agree. The flattest meridian measured 37.12 D on the right and 37.50 D on the left — within 0.4 D of each other, and about 6 D flatter than normative paediatric data: mean keratometry is 43.56 ± 1.96 D across 5,559 children aged 6 to 12 years, 43.28 ± 1.66 D in the six-year-olds 45, and the cornea flattens further with age, having already fallen from a neonatal 51.2 D toward the adult 43.5 D 46. The expected value at three years is therefore at least as steep as the six-year-old figure, making the comparison conservative. This bilateral agreement also sidesteps the one measurement of uncertain reliability: the 4.75 D of astigmatism in the left eye sits in the steep meridian and affects that eye's mean keratometry, not the finding that both flattest meridians lie near 37 D. Both are flatter than any of the twelve eyes in AlBakri's series 5.

An independent clinical observation supports the keratometry. An outpatient note thirteen months earlier, before the right eye had any surgery and before a corneal hypothesis had been entertained, recorded the right cornea as appearing large — large and flat corneas characteristically occur together. Corneal diameter was never measured and topography was not obtainable in an uncooperative child, so the observation could not be quantified; note also that the axial length to corneal radius ratio is derived from the keratometry and is not an independent measurement.

The axial length nonetheless converges with the cohort data: 23.49 and 23.24 mm sit almost exactly on the macular hole subgroup mean of 23.3 mm 10, and roughly 4 mm below the whole-cohort mean of 27.3 mm. Refractive phenotype is not predicted by genotype — the same c.355delG variant appears in a patient whose post-operative refraction was −16.00 D 9.

The intraoperative lens calculation supports a shorter eye still. Pre-operative biometry was acquired through silicone oil, in which sound travels far more slowly than in vitreous, so such measurements are unreliable. Repeat biometry intraoperatively after oil removal raised the required left-eye lens power from a predicted 25.79 D to +30.0 D, and the near-emmetropic post-operative refraction of +1.00 −2.75 × 100 confirmed the higher figure. A four-dioptre discrepancy corresponds to roughly 1.5 mm of axial length, so the recorded values almost certainly overestimate the true ones.

Amniotic Membrane in Vitreoretinal Surgery

Introduced for retinal indications by the Rizzo and Caporossi group around 2019 38 and adopted quickly. Pooled macular hole closure is 94–96%, with no infectious or immunological complication reported across more than 600 grafted eyes 202122.

Mechanism

Structural

Provides a basement-membrane scaffold across a defect that cannot be closed by apposition, and a substrate for glial and neurosensory ingrowth. Optical coherence tomography has shown neuroretinal ingrowth over a plug at one week and a regenerated neurosensory layer covering it at three months.

Antifibrotic

Suppresses transforming growth factor-β signalling and epithelial–mesenchymal transition in exposed retinal pigment epithelium — the pathway driving proliferative vitreoretinopathy 28.

Cellular

In vitro, amniotic membrane significantly reduces proliferation, migration and adhesion of primary human PVR cells and lowers fibrosis marker expression, without toxicity to retinal pigment epithelium, Müller cells or retinal neuroblasts 37.

Immunological

Explanted epiretinal membrane shows no evidence of cellular rejection 37. No systemic or uveitic complication has been reported in any published series 2021.

Placement conventions

Current consensus favours subretinal placement for large, highly myopic or refractory macular holes, and epiretinal placement in proliferative vitreoretinopathy and optic pit maculopathy 23. Reported anatomical success is 80–95% overall: 85–100% for refractory holes, 86–92% for myopic holes with posterior staphyloma, and 76–92% for complex detachment with PVR 23.

Three things this table shows. First, the *72 frameshift appears in Saudi, Turkish, Emirati and UK cohorts under four different nucleotide and codon numbers; if these are one allele, it is the commonest cause of Knobloch syndrome worldwide, and the inconsistent transcripts obscure that. Second, c.2118dup identifies Almasri's patient as Özdek's Patient 25 — same variant, same centre, overlapping authorship — so the two published graft cases are three, not four. Third, the disorder is overwhelmingly truncating: of the variants above only one missense and one synonymous allele appear, which is why a missense finding should prompt caution before the diagnosis is made on genetics alone.

Positioning the Present Case

Four statements are supported by the literature and three are not. Keeping them apart is what separates a claim that survives review from one that does not.

Supported

  • First extramacular amniotic membrane graft in Knobloch syndrome. All 13 previously reported grafts were placed to seal a macular hole 111415.
  • First use of macular and extramacular grafts in the same patient.
  • Only bilateral application known. Stated as a limit of knowledge rather than priority — 44% of the cohort's macular hole cases were bilateral and graft laterality is not published 11.
  • Bilateral oil-free anatomical success. Both previously reported single cases also achieved oil removal 1415; what is new here is that it was achieved in both eyes of one patient.
  • Bilateral symmetrical hyperopia with a corneal rather than axial mechanism — not previously described 45.

Not supported

  • Not the youngest grafting. The cohort's youngest illustrated graft recipient is a 3-month-old 11, and per-patient ages for the 7 amniotic membrane eyes are unpublished.
  • Not the youngest presentation of bilateral macular hole detachment. Alsulaiman's median was 5.5 months, with a 2-month-old 9.
  • Not the first bilateral Knobloch MH-RD. Four of five patients in Alsulaiman's series were bilateral 9.
  • Not evidence that grafting was independently decisive. Encircling buckles, membrane peels, retinectomy, retinotomy with band removal and prolonged silicone oil were all performed — the combination reported to give the best results even without a graft 10.

The three arguments the literature supports

1 · Dual-site grafting is unreported in this disorder

Both prior Knobloch cases grafted the macula only, unilaterally 1415. Extramacular grafting is well established in pathologic myopia and PVR — Lin's posterior paravascular breaks 30, Caporossi's RPE coverage 28, Gu's large patches 3132, and Carlà's statement that epiretinal placement is preferred in PVR 23. It has not been done in Knobloch syndrome, and no patient has received both a macular and an extramacular graft.

2 · The hyperopia is corneal, not axial

Hyperopia has been described only unilaterally in anisometropic patients, attributed to lens subluxation or post-detachment shift 4. Mean keratometry of 37.81 D is flatter than any of the twelve eyes in the biometric reference series (40.00–43.61 D) 5, and the axial length to corneal radius ratios of 2.68 and 2.79 fall below the threshold of 3 that the same paper sets for hyperopia.

3 · The macular hole phenotype favours shorter eyes

Mean axial length was 23.3 mm in macular hole detachment against 26.5 mm in non–macular hole detachment (P = 0.01) 10, and two reported Knobloch eyes were emmetropic at 20.7 and 21.1 mm 4. The present case sits on that subgroup mean rather than outside the syndrome — a documented distribution rather than an anomaly.

What the register shows about outcome

Grafts at the macula seal reliably but rarely improve acuity 1429. The one clear functional exception is a paediatric idiopathic hole in which the graft was tucked subretinally at the hole edge and foveal contour was restored 39. Amniotic membrane may seal without reconstituting retinal tissue: 23.3% of eyes in one series showed foveolar dehiscence with membrane exposed subretinally 33, and graft integration occurred without discernible lamination in every case of another 29. “Covering” is the defensible description; “anatomical closure with restored architecture” usually is not.
